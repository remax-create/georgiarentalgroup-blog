Atlanta House Rents Up 5% While Apartments Slip

Sandra Watkins, Broker/Owner November 27, 2026
Share Facebook LinkedIn X Email

If you own a rental house in metro Atlanta, here's some good news to go with your Thanksgiving leftovers. The newest rent numbers show houses are holding their value with renters, even while apartment rents slide. But there's a catch, and it's all about pricing.

The short version: Zumper's City of Atlanta numbers, updated November 26, show the average house renting for about $2,400, up 5% from a year ago. Two-bedroom rentals are down 2%. Houses are winning, but only when they're priced on what homes actually leased for, not on hope.

The new numbers

Zumper tracks the rentals listed on its site over a rolling 30 days. Here's what the City of Atlanta looked like as of this week:

  • Houses: about $2,400 a month, up 5% from last year
  • 3-bedroom rentals: about $2,300, up 3%
  • 2-bedroom rentals: about $2,002, down 2%

One honest note: these are City of Atlanta numbers, not Canton or Woodstock. Rent for your home depends on your street, your school zone and your home. I use numbers like these to see which way the wind is blowing, not to set your rent.

Why houses are holding up better

Metro Atlanta has built a lot of apartments. In its January 2026 report, Yardi Matrix said developers delivered 16,086 new apartment units in 2025 through November, with another 24,071 under construction at the time. All those new buildings are competing for renters with move-in specials and lower prices.

Single-family rentals don't have that kind of new competition. And a lot of renters still want what an apartment can't give them: a yard, a garage, a little more space, and no neighbor on the other side of the bedroom wall. That's a big reason house rents have kept climbing while apartment rents slipped.

Why the apartment glut still matters to you

Here's the catch. Even if your tenant would never live in an apartment, they're comparing. A renter looking at your three-bedroom house is also looking at a brand-new two-bedroom with a pool, a gym and a move-in special. If your house is priced too far above that, they'll ask themselves whether the yard is worth it.

That's why houses that are clean, updated and priced right are leasing, and houses priced on last spring's hopes are sitting. December and January are slow months for renting, so a home that sits now can sit a while.

How we price a rental house

When we price a home, whether it's a new listing or a renewal, here's what we do:

  • We start with like-kind homes near yours that actually leased in the last few months: similar size, bedrooms, condition and features.
  • We use active listings to see the competition, not to decide what renters will pay. An asking price isn't a lease.
  • We adjust for what makes your home stand out or hold it back: garage, fenced yard, updates, school zone.
  • We set a check-in point. If a home has no applications after about two weeks, we look at the price again with you, so it doesn't sit for months.

For a renewal, we also weigh the cost of a turnover. Keeping a good tenant at a fair rent usually beats chasing the top of the market. I broke that math down in Georgia Tenants Are Staying Longer: Why Renewals Pay.

Quick answers

Are rents going up or down in Atlanta?
It depends on the type of rental. In Zumper's November 2026 City of Atlanta numbers, houses averaged about $2,400, up 5% from a year ago, while 2-bedroom rentals averaged about $2,002, down 2%.

Why are house rents holding up better than apartments?
Metro Atlanta has added a lot of new apartments, and those buildings compete hard for renters. Single-family homes have less new competition, and many renters want a yard, a garage and more space.

How should I price my rental house right now?
Start with like-kind homes near yours that actually leased in the last few months, then adjust for condition and features. Active listings show your competition, not what renters are paying.
