Georgia Eviction Records Can Be Sealed Jan. 1: What Owners Should Know

Sandra Watkins, Broker/Owner November 17, 2026
Share Facebook LinkedIn X Email

For as long as I've been screening tenants, an eviction filing in Georgia has been a public court record that pretty much stays put. That changes on January 1, 2027.

The same law that brought us the new HOA rules, Senate Bill 406, also lets a judge seal certain eviction court records. If you own a rental in Canton, Woodstock or anywhere in Cherokee County, here's what that means for you and how we screen applicants.

The short version: Starting January 1, 2027, a Georgia court can make an eviction (dispossessory) case record unavailable to the public, but only if someone asks, a judge holds a hearing, and the case qualifies. It doesn't erase what a tenant owes, and good screening never depended on court records alone.

What the new rule says

SB 406 adds a new subsection (e) to O.C.G.A. 44-7-50, Georgia's dispossessory statute. Starting January 1, 2027, a court can order all records of an eviction case made unavailable to the public in three situations:

What it does NOT do

  • It isn't automatic. A tenant has to file a motion, and a judge decides after a hearing.
  • It doesn't start yet. Courts can't seal anything under this rule until January 1, 2027.
  • It doesn't wipe out a debt. If a former tenant owes you money, you can still go after it in a separate case or through collections.
  • It doesn't change your lease or the eviction process itself. Notices, filings and timelines work the same way they do today.

Why this matters when we screen applicants

Starting next year, some older or resolved eviction filings may stop showing up on screening reports. For most owners, that's not something to lose sleep over. A case a tenant won, or a judgment they paid off years ago, wasn't the whole story anyway.

What it does mean is that a court-record search can't be the only thing standing between your home and the wrong tenant. That's never how we've done it.

Frequently asked questions

When can Georgia eviction records be sealed?

Starting January 1, 2027. SB 406 adds a new subsection to O.C.G.A. 44-7-50 that lets a court seal dispossessory records on motion and after a hearing.

Are eviction records sealed automatically in Georgia?

No. Someone has to file a motion, and a judge decides after a hearing. The record only qualifies if the tenant won the case, paid the judgment in full with interest and costs, or seven years have passed since the case ended.

Can a landlord still collect money owed if an eviction record is sealed?

Yes. Sealing a record does not erase a debt. A landlord can still pursue money owed through a separate case or collections.
