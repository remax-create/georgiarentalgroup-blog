Freeze-Proof Your Georgia Rental Before the First Cold Snap

Sandra Watkins, Broker/Owner November 20, 2026
Share Facebook LinkedIn X Email

Remember last January? Winter Storm Fern rolled in, Governor Kemp put all 159 Georgia counties under a state of emergency from January 22 through January 29, and wind chills north of I-20 dropped below 10 degrees. Plenty of us spent that week hoping the pipes held.

In a rental, a burst pipe isn't just a mess. It's a water claim, a tenant who may not have a livable home, and a repair bill that always seems to land right around the holidays. The good news: most freeze damage is preventable with about five minutes of work before the first hard freeze.

The short version: Hoses off, spigots covered, crawl space closed up, heat on, and everyone knows where the water shutoff is. Vacant homes need a little extra attention, and your tenants need a heads-up before every hard freeze.

The 5-minute owner checklist

  • Disconnect every garden hose. A hose left attached traps water in the spigot, and that's one of the most common breaks we see.
  • Cover outdoor spigots. Foam faucet covers cost a few dollars at any hardware store.
  • Close crawl space vents (or put covers on them) for the winter, and check that the crawl space door shuts tight.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in the crawl space, garage, attic and along outside walls. Foam pipe sleeves are cheap and quick.
  • Know where the main shutoff is, make sure it actually turns, and label it.

Vacant rentals need extra care

A home between tenants is the one most likely to freeze, because nobody's there to notice a cold house or hear a drip. If your rental is empty this winter:

  • Keep the heat on at a steady temperature. Don't turn it off to save a few dollars. Many insurance policies expect it, so check yours.
  • Leave cabinet doors open under sinks on outside walls.
  • Have someone check the home after a hard freeze, inside and out, so a small leak gets caught before it becomes a ceiling.
  • Ask your plumber whether shutting off the water or draining lines makes sense for a longer vacancy.

What to tell your tenants

Tenants want to protect the home too. They just need a reminder at the right time. Before a hard freeze, send a short note that covers:

  • Let faucets on outside walls drip on the coldest nights.
  • Open the cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks.
  • Keep the heat on, even if they're traveling for the holidays.
  • Where the main water shutoff is, and to call right away if a pipe freezes or leaks.

Put it in writing, by email or text, so everyone has it when they need it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I keep pipes from freezing in a rental house?

Disconnect hoses, cover outdoor spigots, keep the heat on, close crawl space vents for the winter, and have tenants drip faucets and open sink cabinets on the coldest nights. Make sure everyone knows where the main water shutoff is.

What temperature should a vacant rental be kept at in winter?

Keep the heat on and set to a steady temperature. Many insurers and plumbers suggest no lower than about 55 degrees. Check your policy, since some require the heat on for vacant homes.

Who pays for frozen pipe damage in a Georgia rental?

It depends on the cause and your lease. Damage from a failed pipe is usually the owner's repair and insurance claim, while damage caused by a tenant's neglect, like turning the heat off, may be the tenant's responsibility. Your lease and insurance policy decide it.
