Georgia's New HOA Law: What Rental Owners Need to Know

Sandra Watkins, Broker/Owner November 13, 2026
Share Facebook LinkedIn X Email

If your rental sits in an HOA neighborhood in Canton, Woodstock or anywhere in Cherokee County, a new Georgia law is about to change how fines, dues and disputes work. It was signed in May, and most of it starts January 1, 2027.

The good news: most of the changes protect owners. The better news: none of it is hard to handle if you know what's coming.

The short version: Georgia Senate Bill 406, the Georgia Property Owners' Bill of Rights Act, makes HOAs register with the Secretary of State every year, raises the bar for foreclosing on an owner, and gives owners a state complaint process so a fine doesn't mean hiring a lawyer.

The timeline

1. HOAs have to register every year

Starting January 1, 2027, every HOA must register with the Georgia Secretary of State and keep that registration active. If it doesn't, it loses the power to charge fines or fees, file liens or start foreclosure. The Secretary of State is setting up an online portal for this, and we'll check how to look up your HOA as soon as it opens.

2. Foreclosure gets a higher bar

For HOAs covered by the Georgia Property Owners' Association Act, the amount owed before an HOA can foreclose becomes the lesser of $4,000 or 12 months of regular assessments, with a $2,000 minimum. Only regular assessments count. Fines and late fees can't trigger a foreclosure.

The HOA also has to send notice by certified mail at least 60 days ahead, up from 30, and paying the balance in that window ends its right to foreclose.

3. A complaint process that isn't a lawsuit

Owners can file a complaint with the Secretary of State within 180 days of an HOA action that harms them. Filing one puts collection of the disputed fine on hold until a hearing officer decides. The state says it won't act on complaints until January 1, 2027.

What this means for your rental

  • Keep dues current. The new rules protect you, but only a paid-up account is a safe account.
  • Know your HOA's rental rules. Leasing caps, minimum lease terms and tenant registration still come from your community's governing documents. This law doesn't change them.
  • Send us any HOA letter right away. Fines usually start with a tenant's parking, trash cans or a barking dog. The sooner we see the notice, the sooner we fix it with the tenant, and the sooner we can use the new complaint process if the fine isn't fair.
  • Tell us who your HOA contact is. If we have it on file, we can move quickly for you.

Frequently asked questions

When does Georgia's new HOA law take effect?

SB 406 was signed May 12, 2026. Attorney fee rules began July 1, 2026, and HOA registration and the state complaint process begin January 1, 2027.

Can my HOA foreclose on my rental over unpaid fines?

Not under the new law. Fines and late fees can't trigger foreclosure. Only unpaid regular assessments count, and the threshold is the lesser of $4,000 or 12 months of assessments, with a $2,000 minimum.

Does the new law change my HOA's rental restrictions?

No. Leasing caps, minimum lease terms and tenant registration rules still come from your community's governing documents.
