How to Choose a Property Manager in North Georgia: 10 Questions to Ask

Sandra Watkins, Broker/OwnerNovember 12, 2026
Share Facebook LinkedIn X Email

Most of the owners who call me already had a property manager. When I ask why they're switching, the answer is almost always the same: "I never knew what was going on with my house."

A good property manager can make owning a rental easy. The wrong one can make it miserable. Whether you own one home in Canton or several across North Georgia, here are the questions I'd ask before hiring anyone, including us.

10 questions to ask a property manager before you hire them

  1. Are you licensed? In Georgia, managing rental property for others requires a real estate license through the Georgia Real Estate Commission. Ask who the broker is.
  2. Who will I actually talk to? A name and a phone number, not a ticket system. And who answers when your tenant calls at night?
  3. How often will I hear from you? Especially while your home is vacant. You shouldn't have to chase your manager for updates.
  4. Do you charge while my home is vacant?
  5. Do you mark up repairs? And will I see the actual vendor invoice?
  6. Who keeps the late fees?
  7. How do you screen tenants? Credit, background, income, employment, rental history and pets. Ask what gets an applicant denied.
  8. How do you price the rent? On real leased comps, or on a guess? And what happens if the home isn't renting?
  9. How do you handle maintenance and inspections? Who does the work, how are emergencies handled, and how often is the home inspected?
  10. What happens at move-out? How is the home inspected, how are security deposits handled, and how fast is it back on the market?

Red flags to watch for

  • They can't tell you who will manage your home day to day.
  • Fees are vague, or "it depends" on everything.
  • No written answer on repair markups or late fees.
  • No clear screening standards for tenants.
  • You have to call them to find out what's going on.

Frequently asked questions

What should I look for in a property manager?

Look for a licensed broker, clear and complete fees in writing, no markup on repairs, consistent tenant screening, regular inspections and updates without having to ask.

Does a property manager in Georgia need a license?

Yes. Managing rental property for others in Georgia requires a real estate license through the Georgia Real Estate Commission.

How often should a property manager inspect my rental?

It varies by company. Georgia Rental Group does quarterly maintenance inspections to catch small problems before they get expensive.

Can I switch property managers in the middle of a lease?

Usually, yes, depending on your current management agreement. The new manager coordinates the handoff of the lease, security deposit, keys and tenant records.

What would your home rent for?

I'll pull real rental comps and walk you through what you'd likely net each month. No pressure, no obligation.
